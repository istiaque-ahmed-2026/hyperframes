# Plan and review

## Brief to shots

For a multiscene film, write a timecoded shot list with a purpose for each scene, the exact on-screen copy, its source assets and intended transition. Match duration to the brief. For a short advertisement, make the first shot understandable, show the product or result clearly, and leave a readable closing hold.

Choose a consistent type hierarchy, color palette and motion rhythm. Keep action inside the safe area and captions away from essential visual details. Use motion to guide attention. Match transitions to changes of scene or idea.

For Bangla, preserve the supplied spelling, use a local shaping-capable font and inspect conjuncts at phone size. For longer copy, measure and wrap text in the composition rather than forcing a single line.

## Footage, captions and sound

Probe each source and identify duration, rotation, dimensions, audio and color transfer. Confirm ambiguous source-versus-edit timestamps. Derive caption timing from verified speech or supplied cues. Preserve original speech and ambience unless the user requests a change.

Use authorized music and sound. Balance speech against music and inspect for clipping. Generated media depends on the selected available provider, its permissions and the agreed budget. A prompt describing a service is not a completed generation.

## Quality gate

Check the first readable frame, scene transitions, middle of each major scene, longest caption and final hold. Look for text overflow, missing glyphs, incorrect aspect ratio, black gaps, stretched footage and unwanted cropping.

Probe and decode the exported file. Confirm duration, frame rate, dimensions and audio. Listen to the rendered soundtrack if possible. Report any unchecked audio or render properties.

For an existing project, preserve the user's current changes. Make the requested revision, re-render the relevant section and inspect the adjacent cuts. Deliver a fresh output path with a concise description of the applied edit.
